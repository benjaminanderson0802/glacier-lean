# R-GOVDATA — public-record compliance and alert ventures

Research date: 2026-10-10. Prepared for Benjamin Anderson / Anderson Education LLC.

## Drift check and decision frame

- **Checkpoint:** PH12.5, shared government data feeds + deadline tracker. This is an input research report for that checkpoint, not a claim that the block or any venture is implemented.
- **Dependencies:** PH12.5 is `todo`; the owner-approved PH12 wave allows its card to start in parallel. No checkpoint is being marked done.
- **North Star properties / metrics:** P-LOOPS (recurring checks and alerts), P-VERIFY (source/date evidence), P-CONTROL (approval before consequential actions); M-INTERVENE and M-COST are relevant operating measures. No venture revenue target is a North Star metric.
- **Existing solutions / I-01:** Agencies already expose many free databases, APIs, calendars and official reminders. The paid product must add a narrow workflow (entity matching, source/date provenance, escalation, export, cross-source deduplication), not repackage a search page. Research only; no new software or paid tool proposed.
- **Acceptance check (written first):** this file contains 8–12 candidate concepts, a comparative scored top three across the six requested factors, five concrete launch steps for each finalist using the owner's existing accounts where appropriate, and cited primary-source support plus explicit legal/market red flags. This is a research artifact, not evidence that demand or revenue has been proven.

## How to read the scores

Each factor is 1–5. **Demand evidence:** strength of an official obligation, recurring trigger, or observable public record (not proof that buyers will pay). **Competition:** 5 means less direct competition / better differentiation potential; a low score means strong free substitutes or a crowded category. **Setup:** 5 means cheap/simple. **Automation:** 5 means highly automatable after customer setup. **Legal safety:** 5 means lower legal/regulatory risk. **Time to first $:** 5 means plausibly fastest. Scores are judgment calls, not market measurements. Setup estimates assume the owner already has the named accounts, uses free public feeds and local/open-source glue, does not buy ads or data, and keeps total incremental launch spend below $500. Any paid account feature must be turned on only after the owner approves its cost.

### Excluded / rejected directions

- **Mass scraping of city/state licensing sites, county portals, permit portals or logged-in platforms:** reject unless the responsible agency explicitly documents an API/download or grants permission. A public webpage alone is not authorization to crawl it. SEC, for example, expressly says it does not allow unclassified bots and rate-limits automated access; use its designated APIs and obey its developer policy. [SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces), [SEC internet security policy](https://www.sec.gov/about/privacy-information).
- **Generic “all compliance deadlines” product:** too much state-by-state rule interpretation and expensive incumbents; weak wedge and high missed-deadline liability.
- **Public property liens / unclaimed money on contingency:** do not pursue without state-by-state finder-fee, privacy, solicitation and licensing review. The Venture Build Spec explicitly says unclaimed-property finder rules vary by state; this report does not treat public-record access as permission for contingency recovery.
- **Re-selling raw government data as if exclusive:** most cited feeds are already free. Product value must come from a demonstrable niche match/alert workflow and accurate provenance.

### 1. SAM.gov amendment/deadline monitor — recommended first validation

**Why it ranks first.** Government solicitation and amendment records have concrete buyer value when missed, and Benjamin's SAM registration work provides a firsthand workflow. However, SAM itself is free, and alert competitors exist; do not build until contractors confirm that amendments, deadline changes or trade-specific filtering are painful enough to pay for.

**Five-step launch plan (goal: under $100 initial outlay; two bounded owner actions during validation, then routine work is automated):**

1. Choose one narrow trade / NAICS / geography and write a one-page sample digest using only SAM.gov's official opportunities API and published API terms; store notice ID, retrieval time, response date and the exact observed fields. [SAM.gov API documentation](https://open.gsa.gov/api/get-opportunities-public-api/).
2. Use local scripts plus Glacier scheduled flow to poll the API at its allowed rate; compare each notice with the last snapshot collected after onboarding, deduplicate, and emit only “match / no match found as of [date] / uncertain” with source links. The API only returns the latest active version; older versions require SAM Data Services, so do not promise a full pre-enrollment amendment history or build a crawler to recover it. Confirm key/rate terms before production. [API documentation](https://open.gsa.gov/api/get-opportunities-public-api/).
3. Build 10 real example digests for one trade and use the owner's existing Apify Store account only if an Actor format is appropriate; otherwise sell a manual sample via a free landing page. Apify supports monetized Actors and event/usage pricing, but platform usage can create costs, so cap each run and verify unit economics first. [Apify publishing/pricing docs](https://docs.apify.com/actors/running/actors-in-store).
4. Use one 30-minute call with a small contractor group or procurement consultant to test whether buyers will prepay $19–49/month; owner action 1. Do not use bulk automated outreach or imply government affiliation, guaranteed award access or special inside information. SAM is a free public service. [SAM.gov](https://sam.gov/SAM/).
5. If at least two pay or sign a specific paid pilot, launch one paid monthly tier through the existing Apify account / a simple invoice; owner action 2: approve listing and pricing once. Otherwise stop after the call; no ad spend, paid datasets or portal account required.

**Red flags:** government-procurement advice can cross into paid consulting; solicitation matching is not legal eligibility advice. SAM records may be amended or canceled; the public API returns only the latest active version and pre-enrollment changes may require Data Services. Duplicate or stale alerts can be costly. Include date/source and make the user verify the live notice. Registering on SAM is free; third parties charging for basic registration are not official. [SAM API version notes](https://open.gsa.gov/api/get-opportunities-public-api/), [SAM entity checklist](https://sam.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/entity-checklist.pdf).

### 2. EPA ECHO facility change watch — recommended B2B test

**Why it ranks second.** It makes a large public dataset more actionable for consultant and portfolio workflows, with a clear recurring refresh. It is less defensible as a consumer product and must never turn public records into a definitive “compliant/noncompliant” rating.

**Five-step launch plan (goal: $0–100):**

1. Pick a narrow buyer and one geography/program (e.g. environmental consultants tracking a client's NPDES facilities), and interview five practitioners before coding. Present it as a workflow hypothesis, not measured demand.
2. Download EPA's ECHO Exporter / use documented query-only REST services; cache locally, retain source record IDs and refresh timestamp, and compare weekly snapshots. EPA explicitly offers downloads and REST-style web services. [EPA downloads](https://echo.epa.gov/tools/data-downloads), [EPA web services](https://echo.epa.gov/tools/web-services).
3. Build a free sample watchlist for five facilities from public records; send a weekly “new source record / no update found / uncertain” report with source links, not legal conclusions. EPA reports over 1.5 million regulated facilities in the Exporter and provides fields for inspections, violations, actions and penalties. [EPA ECHO downloads](https://echo.epa.gov/tools/data-downloads).
4. Offer three consultants a $49 monthly paid pilot for up to 25 facilities; use the existing Apify account only if users need self-serve runs, otherwise simple manual billing. Keep uploaded client facility lists local and delete them per the Venture Build Spec's retention rule.
5. Require two paid pilots or one repeat renewal before expanding to additional sources. Owner action: approve one paid listing / customer offer. Maintain an error/coverage page stating that ECHO datasets have limitations and that records do not determine liability or current permit status. [EPA source/quality and refresh notes](https://echo.epa.gov/tools/data-downloads).

**Red flags:** ECHO reflects source-system records and refresh cycles, not a real-time regulatory determination. A missing record is not evidence of compliance. A “red flag” score can create defamation, lending, insurance, or procurement exposure; prefer factual event alerts and neutral source language. This category competes with free EPA tools and paid EHS databases.

### 3. USPTO maintenance calendar — only as a lawyer-reviewed reminder tool

**Why it ranks third.** The deadline structure is legible, an official API exists, and the value can be a low-touch portfolio reminder. The agency already sends courtesy reminders, and a missed filing can cancel registration, so the product should sell evidence collection and redundancy—not replace counsel.

**Five-step launch plan (goal: $0–100):**

1. Request an official USPTO TSDR API key and ingest only customer-supplied registration numbers / official API data; obey metering/rate limits. [USPTO TSDR API](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/apply/check-status-view-documents/trademark-bulk-data), [TSDR FAQ/rate limits](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/faqview).
2. Generate a source-linked timeline for 5 sample registrations covering the year 5–6 and year 9–10 statutory windows plus the six-month grace period; require two-engine/script agreement and mark discrepancies uncertain. [USPTO maintenance deadlines](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/maintain/keeping-your-registration-alive).
3. Have a U.S. trademark attorney review the wording and specimen checklist before any public sale. This is one owner action and one paid professional/legal review, so the idea remains below $500 only if the attorney agrees to a short fixed-fee review under that cap; otherwise do not launch.
4. Offer a $39/year reminder pilot to 10 small Shopify merchants or small IP firms, using Benjamin's Shopify Partner account to test a private app only after demand is validated; an email/CSV report avoids premature app review and infrastructure.
5. If two customers pay, launch a reminder-only service with one owner-approved listing/price. Make every customer decide filing content and sign/submit their own USPTO filings; refer legal questions to their attorney.

**Red flags:** The USPTO already emails reminders; the product may have no meaningful willingness-to-pay. Trademark maintenance requires a verified use declaration and specimens; the software cannot decide whether a mark is legally in use or which goods should remain. Incorrect deadline calculations can cause cancellation; Madrid Protocol and special cases need separate handling. [USPTO requirements](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/maintain/keeping-your-registration-alive), [USPTO registration maintenance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/maintaining-registration).

## Recommendation

Validate **SAM opportunity amendment alerts** first because Benjamin can recruit a narrow user sample from the contracting ecosystem and existing account context, with minimal legal interpretation and no paid data dependency. Treat that recommendation as a pre-sale experiment, not a build authorization or proof of demand. If buyers will not prepay for a manually prepared sample digest, stop. EPA ECHO is the fallback if environmental consultants can provide facility lists and pay for dated change detection. Trademark reminders require lawyer-reviewed boundaries and should not be the first offer.
